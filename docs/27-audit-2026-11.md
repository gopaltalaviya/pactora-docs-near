# 27 — Audit of the NEAR build, 2026-11-09

An internal audit of the three NEAR repos on branch `human-work`: the Pactora
contract, the backend that signs for agents and people, and the interface.
It is **not** an external audit, which mainnet still needs
([docs/28](28-feature-inventory-2026-11.md) §6). It is what an external
auditor should no longer find.

Five independent reviewers, each with one angle and no sight of the others'
work:

| Reviewer | Angle | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts, code review | line by line against the spec in docs/04 and the contracts README: funds, state machine, callbacks, access, storage, events | `pactora-contracts-near` bc2f6cf |
| Contracts, property tests | random sequences of up to 120 calls from 16 accounts, 33 kinds of call, 9 invariants checked after every one, exact deadline boundaries, edge values, mutation check | same |
| Contracts, adversarial | real attacks on a local NEAR node across promises: failing callbacks, same-block races, gas sweeps, deposit and storage abuse, DAO misuse, an upgrade over live jobs, end-to-end fund accounting | same, the committed wasm |
| Backend | NEP-413 sign-in, sessions, injection, SSRF, KYC webhooks, key custody, idempotency, indexer, rate limits, uploads, deploy scripts, dependencies | `pactora-backend-near` 074757a |
| Interface | every page in a real browser at 1440 and 390 px: flows, copy, visuals, keyboard, focus, XSS, redirects, token storage, what the wallet is asked to sign, bundle size | `pactora-interface-near` e437ebb |

Every High or Medium claim came with a reproduction: a test, a probe, or a
transaction on the sandbox. Every fix was written test-first: the test fails
on the code before the fix and passes after, and the one line of red evidence
is kept with each finding below. The reviewers' tests were then added to the
repos, so what they found stays found.

## 2. Contracts (`pactora-contracts-near`, now 1.1.0 at `25df5c3`)

### C1 — Medium — a late ruling could be front-run by the worker · **fixed**

`resolve_dispute` had no deadline and `expire_dispute` was permissionless and
allowed from `dispute_deadline + 1`. After the deadline the worker could send
`expire_dispute` in the same block as the arbiter's ruling; whichever landed
first won. With the real Sputnik DAO, the worker could expire the dispute
before the second council vote, and the proposal ended `Failed`. The testnet
dispute timeout was 3600 s against a 7-day DAO proposal period.

Reproduced on the sandbox: in one run the expiry won and the worker was paid
`Unresolved`; in another the ruling won.

Fix: a new parameter `dispute_grace` (testnet 86 400 s, sandbox 10 s).
`expire_dispute` needs `now > dispute_deadline + dispute_grace`; the arbiter
may rule until the expiry actually executes. The testnet `dispute_timeout` is
now 604 800 s, the DAO's proposal period. The grace is read from the current
params at expiry, so no job layout changed.

Red: `tests::audit::a_ruling_after_the_deadline_wins_and_expiry_waits_out_the_grace`
— "expected a panic": the old code let `expire_dispute` succeed at
deadline + 1. Sandbox: `adversarial::racing_transactions_never_pay_twice`.

### C2 — Low — a spending cap could block a client's dispute · **fixed**

`dispute` took the bond through `spend`, which applies the per-task and daily
caps. A client at its daily cap could not dispute, and after the 600 s review
window `auto_approve` paid the worker. SECURITY.md listed it as a known limit.

Fix: the bond is taken by a new `take()` that checks only the balance. The
bond is refunded unless the arbiter rules against the client, so the caps have
nothing to protect.

Red: `audit::a_client_at_its_daily_cap_can_still_dispute` —
`panicked at budget.rs:45:5: DailyCapExceeded`.

### C3 — Low — a dispute the arbiter never answered cost the client nothing · **fixed, a rule change**

On `expire_dispute` the client's bond came back in full and the worker was
paid after waiting the review window plus the dispute timeout, with
`unresolved=1` on its record and no review. The client was not rated. This
matched the spec as written; the owner chose to change it.

Fix: on expiry half of the client's bond goes to the worker, the rest returns
to the client, and `dispute_expired` carries `bond_to_worker` and
`bond_to_client`. docs/04 and the contracts README describe the new rule.

Red: `audit::an_unanswered_dispute_splits_the_bond_between_worker_and_client`
— `left: 990000 right: 1040000` (the worker's balance).

### C4 — Low — `unstaked` was emitted before the payout landed · **fixed**

`complete_unstake` logged `unstaked`, then `transfer_finished{ok:false}` when
the owner was not registered on the token, and the stake went back to
`available` with no event saying so.

Fix: `unstaked` is emitted in `on_send` on success; `stake_restored` on
failure.

Red: `audit::unstaked_is_emitted_only_when_the_payout_lands` — `unstaked`
found in the `complete_unstake` logs; sandbox
`every_failed_transfer_restores_its_bucket` — "unstaked emitted for a payout
that failed".

### C5 — Low — tokens sent with a plain `ft_transfer` were stuck · **fixed**

Nothing could move tokens held above `totals.sum`. Reproduced: 777 tokens
sent by `ft_transfer` sat in the contract forever.

Fix: owner-only, one-yocto `recover_surplus(amount, receiver)`. It reads the
token balance in a promise and, in a private callback, sends
`min(amount, balance − totals.sum)` through the existing `in_flight` /
`on_send` path, so a failed send is restored like every other.

Red: `audit::recover_surplus_takes_only_what_lies_above_the_totals`; sandbox
`the_owner_recovers_only_the_surplus_above_the_totals` — `MethodNotFound`.
The property suite models it: INV-1 is now `sum + surplus == held`.

### C6 — Low — a leaked wallet key can burn the agent's storage NEAR · **documented**

A loop of `create_job` and `cancel` costs about 0.0042 NEAR of storage per
job and `cancel` never gives it back; the 1 NEAR registration deposit was
gone after 235 jobs. Tokens are never lost. The daily cap bounds it to
daily cap ÷ minimum job amount per day (100 jobs, about 0.42 NEAR, at the
testnet defaults). Kept as a known limit in SECURITY.md.

### C7 — Info — items fixed

- `new` is `#[private]` in both contracts; `deploy.mjs` creates the account
  in one transaction and deploys and initialises as the contract account in
  the next.
- A zero accept or work window reports `InvalidWindow`, not `WindowTooLong`
  (red: `expected "InvalidWindow", got "WindowTooLong"`).
- The state has a `version` field and a `#[private] #[init(ignore_state)]
  migrate()` that reads the 1.0.0 layout from the raw `STATE` bytes and
  refuses to run twice. Test: old bytes → `migrate` → 17 views identical.
  The sandbox upgrade test deploys the old wasm, creates jobs in every
  state, then deploys the new wasm and `migrate` in one batch; every open
  job settles on the new code.
- The repo's own suite missed two exact-boundary mutants (`accept_job` at
  exactly the deadline, `expire_undelivered` at exactly the deadline); the
  new `fuzz_boundaries_exact` catches both.
- CI runs the adversarial suite with the previous wasm
  (`wasm/previous/pactora.wasm`) and the fetched Sputnik wasm.

### C8 — Info — items documented, not changed

- Jobs can be created that a worker on default caps can never accept (the
  bond counts against its per-task cap). The client cancels or the accept
  window expires; nothing is trapped.
- `storage_deposit` and `storage_withdraw` reuse NEP-145 names with other
  signatures; a NEP-145 call panics and its deposit is refunded.
- Methods that send tokens need about 35 Tgas; near-cli's default 30 fails
  cleanly.
- A recreated account name takes over a deleted owner's agent. This is
  NEAR's naming model.
- Code upgrades are governed by the contract account's full-access key, not
  the in-contract owner. Removing that key or putting deploys behind the
  DAO is a mainnet prerequisite (docs/28 §6).
- A frozen client cannot dispute during the review window.

### What held

Every attack below was run on a real node against the committed wasm and
failed:

- outsiders calling `on_send`, `on_storage_sent` or `ft_on_transfer`; a
  look-alike token;
- failed transfers to nonexistent, unregistered or deleted accounts: each
  restored its bucket, `in_flight` back to 0;
- same-block races: two withdraws, approve vs dispute, two `claim_fees`, two
  `complete_unstake`, two `storage_withdraw`: exactly one succeeded;
- gas sweeps from 3 to 300 Tgas: at no level did state commit while the
  callback could not run; no method uses more than 3.5 Tgas, no loop is
  unbounded;
- 16 of the 17 one-yocto methods refuse 0 and 2 yocto in the deposit sweep
  (`exact_yocto_rules_and_overpayment_is_refundable`; the seventeenth,
  `accept_owner`, is exercised in the ownership tests); all 8 non-payable
  methods refuse 1 yocto; overpayment is refundable to the last yocto
  (**corrected 2026-11-09:** this said 15);
- storage spam stopped by `InsufficientStorage`; the contract's own NEAR
  never fell;
- strangers on every owner, pauser and agent-owner method; re-initialising;
  ruling twice, on a non-disputed job, or after the arbiter changed;
- every job event's fields match the indexer's decoder exactly; failed
  receipts emit nothing the indexer reads;
- end to end, after every flow and a full withdrawal: totals 0, contract
  holds 0 tokens, supply conserved.

The property suite (`contracts/pactora/src/fuzz.rs`) predicts success or
refusal before every call and the full post-state after it, and checks nine
invariants after every step: conservation, bucket sums, stake locks, job
shape, `verdict_of`, records and reviews, the wallet index, storage balances,
legal transitions only. 256 cases of up to 120 calls per run — the default in
`fuzz.rs` (`cases()`), which CI uses unchanged since `ci.yml` sets no
`PROPTEST_CASES`; set it higher for a longer run — no violation. Mutation
check: five broken guards and arithmetic lines each caught. (**Corrected
2026-11-09:** this said 100 in CI and 256 in a longer run.)

Gate after the fixes: unit 63, sandbox 6/6, adversarial 14/14, fuzz 256
cases, `cargo fmt` and `clippy -D warnings` clean, `verify-near` on a fresh
sandbox 26 checks, 28 with `--dao`. New wasm sha256:
`pactora.wasm` `e0ed7876953624c49bbee99e4f4be2a9b5386c623f7b036180506388d1d2290a`,
`mock_usdc.wasm` `de2a79c2e053ca08f346e629ce253c12c0c3ee3f869f452e8223606c02a1c0d0`.

## 3. Backend (`pactora-backend-near`)

Now `aa8dfa3`. Every fix has a red-first test; the auditor's three
reproduction tests were adapted into the suite.

### B1 — High — one retried hire could be signed and executed twice · **fixed**

The signer treated `InvalidNonceError` from `send_tx` as "the node refused
it, never included", marked the row `failed` with no hash and told the caller
a retry was safe. But near-api-js's provider re-sent the same signed
transaction up to 12 times on a 500, 503, 408 or a dropped connection. A
transaction that landed while its answer was lost was re-sent, the re-send
answered `InvalidNonce`, the caller retried with the same idempotency key,
and the signer signed a second `create_job` with a fresh nonce: a double
escrow from the purse, bounded only by the on-chain caps.

Reproduced through the production `nearClient` against the fake RPC: two
`create_job` transactions on chain, nonces 5000001 and 5000002.

Fix: `send_tx` goes through a one-attempt provider. On any rejection the
signer first asks `txOutcome(hash)`, then reads the key's nonce: a known
outcome is final or reverted; a nonce at or above ours, or an unreadable
key, keeps the row `signed` and hands it to `resolveUncertain`; only a nonce
below ours means `failed`.

Red: `apps/signer/test/signer.test.ts` — `expected 'failed' not to be
'failed'` (two `create_job`s on chain); `expected 4 to be 1` sends.

### B2 — Medium — wallet-key creation stalled the signer and left junk rows · **fixed**

`POST /v1/agents/wallet-key` ran a synchronous scrypt (N = 2^15) on the
signer's event loop and stored a row for any name at all. Thirty requests
stalled the loop 201 ms and left thirty rows that `WalletWatch` then read
from the RPC on every interval, unbounded. At the API's 600/min this kept
the loop saturated and delayed every `/sign`.

Fix: async `crypto.scrypt`; the route is limited to 10/min per client
(`WALLET_KEY_RATE_PER_MINUTE`); `WalletWatch` reads at most 8 accounts at
once and only wallets an `agents` row names; an hourly sweep deletes keys
older than a day whose account is not on chain and no agent names
(`SIGNER_KEY_UNCLAIMED_GRACE_MS`), keeping everything when the RPC fails.
This also closes the known issue "anyone can create wallet keys for accounts
that do not exist, which turns `/v1/status` degraded".

One deviation from the auditor's suggestion: the key is **not** gated on
the account existing on chain, because registration creates the account
with that key (`CreateAccount` + `AddKey` in one transaction), so it cannot
exist first.

Red: `apps/signer/test/keys-dos.test.ts` — `the event loop stalled 217 ms`;
`expected 40 to be <= 8` RPC reads; `sweepUnclaimed is not a function`.

### B3 — Medium — unauthenticated key re-issue cost up to 20 RPC reads · **fixed**

Re-issuing an agent key without a `nonce` tried every live challenge, up to
20, and each `verifyNep413` read the access key over the RPC before checking
the signature. Twenty garbage signatures caused twenty RPC reads and no local
rejection; at 600/min that is 12 000 reads a minute per client against the
shared RPC. `/v1/session` and `/v1/agents` had the same order, one read each.

Fix: `verifyNep413` checks the ed25519 signature locally first and reads the
RPC only on success; `nonce` is required and the loop is gone; both re-issue
routes are limited to 20/min (`REISSUE_RATE_PER_MINUTE`).

Red: `packages/near/test/near.test.ts` — `expected 20 to be +0` RPC reads;
`expected 201 to be 422` without a nonce.

### Info

Fixed: backups carry an HMAC-SHA256 beside the ciphertext and
`restore-test.sh` verifies it before `pg_restore` (a flipped byte failed the
MAC while openssl still decrypted without error); AES-GCM tags must be 16
bytes and the stored scrypt cost is bounded (a 4-byte tag and N = 2^18 were
accepted before); a channels outbox test that failed one run in three
(rows took the database clock while the test froze its own) now passes
10/10.

Documented, not changed: the dispute reason is public in `job.disputed`
(chat words are withheld; this is a design choice to confirm); a session
outlives the removal of the key that made it, up to 7 days, and admin
actions keep their 1-hour rule; the API and signer share one database role.

Dependencies: `pnpm audit --prod` clean. Seven moderate advisories are in
dev tooling only (esbuild, vitest, glob, minimatch), none in a production
image; the fix is vitest 5, a major jump, not taken now.

### Contract 1.1.0 follow-through

`dispute_grace` in the on-chain params schema and `chain/config`; the keeper
reads it from `get_params` each sweep and does not try `expire_dispute`
inside the grace (red: it sent `expire_dispute` at deadline + 1);
`/v1/network` exposes `windows.disputeGrace`; the indexer decodes the new
`dispute_expired` fields (nullable for 1.0.0 events) and keeps both
`dispute_bond_settled` events.

single-use nonce consumed with the session in one transaction); token
prefixes, hashing and constant-time compare; the admin and reviewer 1-hour
rule, recusal, stale evidence refusal; the KYC webhook HMAC with a ±300 s

redirects; all SQL parameterised; the signer's method allow-list and
per-method schemas, per-wallet lock, nonce never reused, AES-256-GCM keys

## 4. Interface (`pactora-interface-near`, now `bfc522a`)

Every finding has a test in `e2e/audit.spec.ts` or a unit file; on the old
build the spec ran **25 failed, 1 passed** (the one pass is the negative
guard for M6).

### I1 — High — the browser signed whatever the API prepared · **fixed**

`lib/person-action.ts` and `AdminView.tsx` sent `receiverId`, `methodName`,
`args` and `deposit` from `prepare` straight to the wallet; only `signerId`
was checked. A compromised or misconfigured API could ask for `ft_transfer`
to an outside account under a button that said "Approve and pay".

Fix: `lib/wallet-guard.ts`. The receiver must be the Pactora contract (or
the token, only for `ft_transfer_call` into escrow); the method must be the
one the action expects; `job_id` must equal the job's chain id when known;
the deposit must be 0 or 1 yocto (a DAO proposal bond at most 1 NEAR); a
fund's amount must equal the job's. Anything else is refused before the
wallet opens: "Pactora asked the wallet to sign something unexpected …
refused".

Red: `lib/person-action.test.ts` — "promise resolved instead of rejecting"
(it signed a hostile `ft_transfer`).

### I2 — High — typed API keys stayed in a page variable · **fixed**

The early-input script in `app/layout.tsx` recorded every input before
hydration and was never removed; `Field.tsx` cleared its entry once. An API
key typed into a password field after load sat in `window.__pactoraEarly`
for the life of the tab, readable by any same-origin script or extension.

Fix: the script skips `type=password`, and the first mounted field removes
the listener and the global.

Red: `{"_R_5fivbb_":"ak_typed_after_hydration"}` recorded.

### I3 — High — the desktop header broke at every usual width · **fixed**

"Live demo" wrapped onto two lines at 1024, 1280 and 1440; signed in,
"Register an agent" wrapped into three lines inside a 32 px button; signed
out, "Sign in" wrapped. Fix: `whitespace-nowrap shrink-0` on the header
items, "Demo", the search label from xl, the register CTA from xl. Red: text
on 2 lines, CTA 40 px in 32 px.

### Low

Fixed, each with a test: confirmations on bid withdraw, photo withdraw and
the Reject vote; "Fund and stake" on both sign-in and /me; the phone menu
shows one of Sign in / My account; 24 px minimum tap targets on copy buttons,

### What held

No horizontal overflow on any of 110 page × viewport × sign-in
combinations; one h1 and a title everywhere; no unlabeled input. Tab order
is logical with visible focus; the search palette and the phone menu trap
and return focus. Forms show specific errors with `role=alert` and
`aria-describedby`. All user text is rendered as React text; the one
`dangerouslySetInnerHTML` is the static layout script; photos are fetched as
blobs and hash-checked. Outbound links go through `safeHref`; route ids are
validated; the session token is in sessionStorage only. Shared JS is 102 kB,
the largest route 135 kB.

Gate after the fixes: 290 unit tests; e2e in all five browsers, then a clean
rerun of the affected suites, 761 passed, 0 failed, 14 skipped by design.

## 6. Not covered

- The real wallets (Meteor, Intear) were not driven; the scripted test
  wallet was.
- The KYC held and blocked pages were reviewed in code only; the live suite
  (`e2e-live/`) covers them against the real backend.
- Whether a real nearcore or a load-balanced RPC answers a duplicate
  `send_tx` with `InvalidNonce` is plausible, not proven; the fix does not
  depend on it.
- The property suite runs in the mocked runtime, so `#[private]`,
  `#[payable]` refusals and `#[init]` are covered by the sandbox tests only.
- An external audit before mainnet (docs/28 §6).
