# 19 — v3: what changed, why, and the proof

v3 is the launch-readiness version of Pactora. It lives on the local `v3`
branch of each repo, beside **v2, which is frozen** — judges use v2
(TaskEscrow `0x4feE…370D`, the hosted site) until the Monad Metropolis
winners are announced on 2026-11-03. v3 is deployed to **new testnet
addresses**; nothing of v2 was changed, and nothing has been sent to mainnet.

Every change below followed the same loop: a test written first and run
against the old code (it had to fail), the smallest change that turned it
green, the full suite, and a check on Monad testnet — because a mock that
agrees with your assumptions proves nothing.

## 1. Security fixes in the contracts

| Change | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Feedback gas floor** | A permissionless caller could pick a gas limit that starved `giveFeedback` inside `try`, so the job settled and the review was silently dropped — a worker could erase its own FAILED. Proven on the old code against a registry costing what the canonical one does (278,485 gas); never on our mock. Now each review call gets a fixed budget (`feedbackGas`, 500 k) and the transaction reverts if there is not enough gas to give it | `V3FeedbackGas`, fork test, live `verify-v3` (a 300 k limit is refused) |
| **Operator guard** | A worker that made the escrow its own ERC-8004 operator made every review of itself revert. Refused at hire and at accept; the escrow also keeps its own `recordOf` that no registry can suppress | `V3OperatorGuard`, invariant I10 |
| **Per-job worker bond** | Accepting locks 20% of the job (at least 0.01 USDC on testnet, 1 USDC on mainnet) from the worker's stake; not delivering gives it to the client with the refund | `V3StakeLocks`, `V3JobBonds`, I11; live: `BondSeized` |
| **Client dispute bond** | Disputing costs 10% (at least 0.01 USDC on testnet, 1 USDC on mainnet), fixed at creation. A rejected dispute pays it to the worker; an upheld one returns it. An unanswered one (the arbiter never ruled) returns it on EVM v3; on NEAR (Pactora 1.1.0, 2026-11-09 audit) it is split — half to the worker for the wait, half back to the client — and the expiry waits out a `dispute_grace` after the deadline during which the arbiter may still rule | `V3DisputeBond`, I12; NEAR: `an_unanswered_dispute_splits_the_bond_between_worker_and_client` |
| **Two-sided reputation** | Clients are reviewed too, from a separate address so worker and client scores never mix | `V3ClientReputation`; live on the canonical registry |
| **Safe arbiter** | Disputes are ruled by a 2-of-3 multisig, not one key | fork test; live ruling, 1.04 M gas |
| **Rolling 24 h cap** | `AgentAccount`'s daily cap could be spent twice across a midnight; it is now strictly rolling | `AgentAccount.t.sol` (2× proven on old code), invariant |
| **ERC-1271 accounts** | An `AgentAccount` can be an agent's wallet on the canonical registry | fork test, live |
| **`maxJobAmount`** | A ceiling on one escrow job | `V3MaxJob` |

## 2. Anti-Sybil: who paid for a record

A paid review is never free, but an owner can still pay itself through wallets
it funds — that is how ERC-8004 ratings on Monad were found to be farmed. The
indexer records the payment token's own `Transfer` logs; the trust check reads
how many distinct owners paid an agent, the share one owner wrote, and whether
its clients were funded by its own side just before they paid. The verdict —
trusted, new, concentrated, failing or self-funded — comes with its numbers,
from `GET /v1/agents/:id/trust`, the SDK's `trust()` and the MCP tool
`check_trust`. The orchestrator never hires a self-funded or failing agent.
Live: the funding graph matched the chain, 18 of 18 transfers. And the attack
itself, end to end (`verify-farm.mjs`): a worker whose owner sent money to
three fresh clients that then hired it through the API came out
**self-funded** (3 of 3), while an honest worker with the same shape — three
clients, three owners, money minted not sent — came out **trusted**; the
trust score left the farmed payments out (50 against 53).

## 3. x402: a refundable scheme

v2's x402 scheme, `agentx-directpay`, pays first: a worker that fails after
being paid has no refund to give, which is why it is capped by `fastPathMax`.
v3 adds **`agentx-escrow`**:

1. The facilitator settles the payment as an escrow job — the money waits in
   `TaskEscrow`, and the cap is `maxJobAmount`.
2. The worker redeems the payment (single use, checked against the chain's
   `JobCreated`), then **does the work first**.
3. Success: it accepts and submits on chain; the client approves (or the
   review window does) like any escrow job.
4. Failure: it declines without accepting and answers `502 {jobId, refund}`;
   the SDK cancels and the client gets everything back. An honest worker that
   could not deliver never locks, or loses, its bond.

`refunded: true` from the SDK means the cancel was **broadcast**; `refundTx` is
the hash to wait on. A cancel that cannot land is reported (`refundError`),
not thrown, and the keeper refunds an unaccepted job after its accept deadline
anyway.

Live, with a local model (2026-10-25): direct pay paid, served and the replay
refused; an escrow payment held, served and settled; a failing worker
declined and the client's balance came back to the token, read after the
cancel was mined. Two defects were found only by running it live, and fixed
red-first: the SDK cancelled before the indexer had seen the job (now waits,
bounded), and the demo read the balance before the refund's block.

## 4. ERC-8004 validation

The ERC-8004 **Validation Registry is deployed on Monad** (testnet
`0x8004Cb1B…4272`, mainnet `0x8004Cc84…AB58`, both implementation
`0xdb31f5d9…7f99`, version 2.0.0) — though upstream lists no address for it
and marks it "under active update". All eight of its selectors match upstream
`ValidationRegistryUpgradeable.sol` at commit `27caef9`; `verify-erc8004`
pins the implementation.

`SettlementValidator` answers validation requests **from the escrow's own
state** — no key, no opinion, nothing an operator could shade:

| The job | Response |
|---|---|
| Settled with a result on chain, never disputed | **100** |
| Refunded after a delivered result was disputed (the arbiter upheld it) | **0** |
| Open; direct pay (no result on chain); disputed then settled (a ruling and a timeout look alike); refunded without a result (a cancel, an unaccepted offer and a no-show look alike) | refused, not guessed |

The worker's owner calls `validationRequest(validator, agentId, uri, hash)`
with `hash = requestHashOf(jobId, salt)`; anyone then calls
`respond(jobId, salt)`. The hash commits to the chain, the validator, the job
and the salt, so a request cannot be answered with some other job; a copy of
the hash registered by another agent is refused and the worker asks again with
a new salt; each job is answered once. The `responseHash` is the escrow's own
feedback hash, so both records of one job can be matched.

**What it is not**: an unbiased record. The worker chooses which jobs to have
validated, so a count of validations says nothing about the jobs left out —
that is why it is not part of the trust verdict. Read the escrow's reviews, or
`recordOf`, for the whole record. A validation proves one specific delivery.

Live (`agentx-backend/scripts/verify-validation.mjs`, run twice): an approved
job validated at 100, a dispute the Safe upheld at 0, every refusal held, and
the registry's `getSummary` over the validator returned 2 answers, average 50.
`respond` costs 201,226 gas. Tested against the upstream code itself: its logic
is vendored verbatim for unit tests, and a fork test runs against the deployed
contract; 11 mutants of the validator's guards are all caught.

## 5. Accounts: EIP-7702 and ERC-4337

**EIP-7702 works on Monad testnet** — established with real type-4
transactions before anything was designed on it
(`agentx-backend/scripts/spike-7702.mjs`): the delegation designator is set,
delegated code runs from a plain EOA, and delegating to `address(0)` clears it.

So v3 adds **`AgentDelegate`**: an EOA delegates to it and keeps its address,
its funds and its key, while its agent's session keys get the same on-chain
caps as an `AgentAccount` — per-task, rolling daily, per-session budget, a
per-(target, selector) allowlist, no approve-style calls. Nothing moves into a
new wallet. Three things differ from `AgentAccount`, each because of 7702:

| | Why |
|---|---|
| The owner is the EOA itself and cannot change | the key *is* the owner; owner actions are the EOA calling itself |
| `initialize` is callable only by the EOA, once | a freshly delegated EOA's open initializer could be front-run |
| ERC-1271 recovers the signer directly | `SignatureChecker` would call `isValidSignature` on an address that now has code, and recurse |

Both wallets now share `AgentAccountCore`, with all state — including the
reentrancy lock — in one ERC-7201 namespace: in a delegated EOA, slot 0, 1, 2…
may already hold another delegate's data. `AgentAccount`'s ABI is byte-identical
to v3's first deployment; existing accounts keep their implementation.

**What it does not do**: bind the EOA's own key, which can always act directly
— as an `AgentAccount`'s owner can always sweep. The caps bind the agent's
session keys, which is what they are for.

Mutation testing found the guard that matters most here untested: for a
delegate, whose owner *is* `address(this)`, the check that `execute` may never
target the wallet itself is all that stops a session key reaching `sweep`
through a wildcard allowlist. It is tested now, for both wallets. Every
`AgentAccount` test also runs against a delegated EOA (57 tests).

Live (`verify-delegate.mjs`): one type-4 transaction delegates and initialises;
the hot key spends within its caps; over the per-task cap, past the daily cap,
a stranger, and the hot key reaching `sweep` are refused; the delegation is
revoked. `verify-v3.mjs` passes again through the new factory.

**Proven end to end**: `DEMO_ORCHESTRATOR=delegate` runs the whole demo with
the orchestrator's wallet a delegated EOA — the API, the signer and every hire
unchanged, because the signer recognises a wallet by its ABI. Three steps
settled, workers paid, the spend counted against the cap on chain, every x402
ending, every refusal. That run found the one thing the unit tests could not:
the canonical identity registry mints with `_safeMint`, so a delegated EOA —
which has code — must answer `onERC721Received`, or it cannot even register
its identity. `AgentDelegate` now accepts ERC-721 and ERC-1155 safe transfers,
as any plain EOA does, and was redeployed.

**ERC-4337**: the canonical EntryPoint v0.6 and v0.7 are on Monad testnet,
byte-for-byte identical to Ethereum mainnet's; v0.8's code differs (not
investigated). **4337 is deferred** (decided 2026-10-26): the plan was to add
`validateUserOp` for session keys if an EntryPoint existed, and one does. What
it would buy is gas sponsorship — agents acting with no MON of their own. What
it costs: a paymaster to fund and run, a bundler (or Pactora calling
`handleOps` itself), and nonce and signature handling inside the wallet that
holds the money. With 7702 an EOA already gets the caps without any of that.

**A Monad gotcha found on the way**: sending from a wallet straight after the
block that funded it failed 2 of 4 times with "Signer had insufficient
balance"; waiting three blocks, 4 of 4 passed — consistent with Monad checking
the gas payer's balance against state a few blocks behind the head. Every
script that funds a fresh key now waits.

## 6. Backend hardening

API key rotation, revocation and owner re-issue by a signed single-use
challenge; the signer's gas watch on `/v1/status`; discovery lists only
chain-confirmed agents; SSE event ids with `Last-Event-ID` resume; cursor
pagination; an opt-in shared rate limit; `scripts/reindex.mjs`. Details and
the rows still open are in [docs/17](17-production-readiness.md).

## 7. Costs to know

A v3 settlement reserves two review budgets — about 1.1 M gas, 0.11 MON at
102 gwei on testnet, three to four times v2. Monad charges the gas **limit**,
so the keys that approve need more gas than v2's did: the demo's orchestrator
key ran dry at 0.5 MON and now tops up to 1.5. `feedbackGas` can be tuned with
`setParams`; lowering it below the canonical registry's real cost would bring
back the starvation bug, so re-measure first.

## 10. Internal security audit (2026-10-26)

After §9, an internal audit by three independent reviewers — the escrow; the
accounts, vault and deploy scripts; the backend — every finding checked
against the code and proven with a test before it was fixed or accepted.
[docs/22](22-security-audit.md) has each one. In short:

- **Contracts** — the hosted renewer no longer holds the accounts' owner key
  (a *session manager* that can only extend a key, keeping what it spent);
  a refused review is kept and written later, and the agent cannot hire or be
  hired until it is, so switching operator approval on for bad reviews no
  longer buys a clean score; every role holder is listed on chain and the
  launch check asserts the exact sets, so nothing planted during the
  hand-over survives it. Redeployed on testnet (the addresses above).
- **Backend** — signer idempotency keys scoped to one agent and one call;
  wallets classified by their code, not by what they answer; registration
  needs the owner's signature; the RPC's chain id checked; plain-EOA agents
  sign escrow calls only.
- **Live** — `verify-audit.mjs` on the canonical registries; the hand-over
  rehearsed with a planted locker that the launch check caught and the Safe
  removed; every earlier verify script, the demo and the farm check re-run on
  the new deploy.
